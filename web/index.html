A trackpad that feels like one

Slow fingers get precision, fast ones cross the screen. Two-finger scrolling with momentum, pinch, tap and drag, three and four finger swipes. A lost Wi-Fi packet never loses movement.

Shortcuts you record, not spell

Press the keys on your laptop and they land on your phone as a deck key. Chain several to run in order. Or tap them together from a key grid.

Point from across the room

Hold the button and move the phone. It reads gravity, so it works however you hold it. Slides mode adds a timer, a laser and one very large Next.

Your laptop's files, in hand

Browse, search and preview. Save a file to your phone, take a whole folder as a zip, or open something on the laptop. Deleting only ever means the Recycle Bin.

Send things without thinking

Share from any app, or drop files on the SyncPad window. You see the progress and speed, and you can cancel. The clipboard can follow you too.

Turn it sideways and play

Over Bluetooth, Windows sees a real controller with no driver. Over USB and Wi-Fi it presses keyboard keys instead.

A deck you can press right now.

On your phone these keys run shortcuts, open programs or type for you. Here they run this page.

Pair it once.

  1. Open SyncPad.exe on your laptop. It shows a code.
  2. Scan the code with the phone app. The code pairs one phone, then changes.
  3. Swipe. Next time it connects by itself.

It can control your computer, so it had better be careful.

Encrypted end to end
Keystrokes, files and screen travel under AES-256-GCM with fresh keys for every connection. Bluetooth uses Bluetooth's own encryption.
Nothing in the middle
No account and no analytics. Your phone talks to your laptop directly. Away from home, free iroh servers help them find each other and may pass along encrypted traffic they cannot read. Update checks ask GitHub for the newest version number, and you can turn them off.
One key per phone
Exchanged by the pairing code and protected by Windows on the laptop. Only phones paired at home can reach it from elsewhere. Press Forget and that phone is locked out.
You can see it
Windows notifies you when a phone pairs, and SyncPad says when a phone is viewing your screen or recording a shortcut.
Open source
MIT licensed, with the protocol written down and the tests included. Read the code.

What it does not do

  • It is for Android 9 or newer and Windows 10 or 11. There is no iPhone or Mac version.
  • From another network it goes direct when both routers allow. Otherwise it uses a relay, which is slower, and the free relays are shared and rate limited.
  • Windows keeps some things to itself: Win+L, Ctrl+Alt+Del, and programs running as administrator unless SyncPad is too.
  • Some phone hotspots pause their radio, which no app can fix. SyncPad tells you when it happens.
  • There is no forward secrecy yet. Someone who records your traffic and later steals the key could read it.

Good to know

Which connection should I use?

USB when you want it smoothest. Bluetooth when wireless, or on a computer you cannot install things on. Wi-Fi when you want files or the screen without a cable. In Automatic, SyncPad picks for you, and uses the internet only when nothing nearby answers.

Does it work when the phone is not on my Wi-Fi?

Yes. Pair at home once, then SyncPad reaches the laptop from another Wi-Fi or mobile data, as long as the laptop is on and online. It goes direct when the networks allow, which is about as quick as the internet between them. Otherwise it goes through a relay and says so. You can turn this off on either device.

It stays on Searching over Wi-Fi.

Campus, hotel and guest networks often stop devices from seeing each other. Connect the laptop to your phone's hotspot, or use USB or Bluetooth. Also check that Windows Firewall allows SyncPad on that kind of network.

USB needs debugging turned on. Why?

SyncPad reaches the laptop through Android's debugging link, which needs adb on the laptop. USB tethering also works and needs no debugging.

Does it work on the lock screen?

Over Bluetooth, yes: to Windows it is an ordinary mouse. Over USB and Wi-Fi, Windows does not let programs reach the lock screen.

Can the laptop see my phone's files?

Only if you turn on Phone files and grant Android's permission. Then it can browse and add files while SyncPad is open. It cannot delete any.
